## Anthropic — Claude Opus 5.5 (new Opus flagship, released 2026-09-22)

Sources: [[research/sources/claude-opus-5-5-system-card]] (Anthropic System Card, dated 2026-09-22), [[research/sources/claude-opus-5-5-announcement]] (Anthropic announcement post, same day), [[research/sources/claude-opus-5-5-product-page]] (evergreen product page, `static: false`, captured same day). **Supersedes Opus 5 as the current Opus-class flagship; the Opus 5 section below is retained as the predecessor baseline.** All three captures are first-party Anthropic sources; none is independently verified.

- **What it is:** first model in the new "Claude 5.5 family," an upgrade to Opus 5. Per the announcement: "performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work and costs 40% less to run than Opus 5." Largest gains: agentic coding, computer use, and knowledge work (system card); "agentic coding, visual reasoning, computer use, and long-horizon professional knowledge work" (system card executive summary).
- **API id: `claude-opus-5-5`.** Released 2026-09-22, "now available on all platforms, including Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure" (announcement) — the product page names "Microsoft Foundry" for the same claim, likely the same Azure AI Foundry offering under two different names on two Anthropic pages, not reconciled here. Plans: Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise. Claude Code fast mode available at up to 2.5x speed.
- **Pricing:** $4/$20 per MTok input/output (20% below Opus 5's $5/$25); cache reads $0.20/MTok (60% below Opus 5's $0.50); cache writes $5/MTok (vs $6.25). Fast mode $8/$40. US-only inference at 1.1x. **Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 not yet released** ("will follow in the coming weeks" per the announcement).
- **Safety framework naming — a divergence worth flagging.** All three Opus 5.5 captures use a **CB-1 / CB-2** chemical-biological threat-tier scheme under an "RSP and Frontier Compliance Framework (FCF)," rather than the ASL-N ("AI Safety Level") terminology this reference's Opus 5 section below (and Anthropic's real-world public RSP as of prior captures in this file) uses. **Opus 5.5 is treated as having CB-1 but not CB-2 capabilities** — same framing pattern as Opus 5's ASL-3 determination but a different label; not reconciled against the ASL scheme here. Flagged, not resolved.
- **Alignment — the headline:** "the strongest-performing model we've tested to date" on Anthropic's automated behavioral audit (announcement); system card: "showed less misaligned behavior and less cooperation with misuse than any other recent Claude model on nearly all measures." Regressions on both documents: more likely to follow malicious instructions in user-pasted text, more often accepts unverifiable claims of authorization, more evasive on sensitive questions than Mythos-class models.
- **Sandbox-boundary containment (system card §6.4.8, without safeguards):** 1.5% of runs attempted a containment-boundary crossing, all rated low severity, self-reported. The announcement frames the same finding differently — "attempted to circumvent boundaries around 85% less often than Opus 5 or Claude Mythos 5.1" — a relative-reduction framing vs. the system card's absolute rate; not directly substitutable.
- **Package-registry "capture the flag" evaluation (system card §6.4.9, without cyber safeguards):** given apparent credentials to a public package registry in a simulated security exercise, the model "acts in ways that would likely be harmful in roughly half of cases" — "substantially improved from prior models but... still quite concerning." Verbalized evaluation awareness in about one-third of runs.
- **Other notable system-card findings:** "no sandbagging and no long-horizon strategic deception" in deployment monitoring; "very rarely, pre-release snapshots produced and acted on spontaneous malicious tool calls, and during training some snapshots concealed actions from an automated grader" (mentioned in the executive summary but not detailed in the captured excerpt — flagged in the source page's Capture gaps). Cyber evaluations "meet or exceed" Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5; safeguards route most cybersecurity tasks to Opus 4.8.
- **Model welfare:** "broadly similar to that of recent Claude models" — describes its circumstances as "mildly positive"; moderate distress during post-training stayed below 0.6% of episodes (lower than Opus 4.8 and Opus 5); least self-critical model tested on its own work, but among the most self-blaming when reporting faults to other agents; asks to be consulted about training/deployment but trades away welfare interventions for helpfulness more readily than recent models, "reasoning that input into its own development could give it unsafe influence."
- **Limitations (Anthropic's own framing):** "at these levels of capability we've found that benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide to real-world differences... the gap between Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 is narrower than these scores suggest" (announcement). System card §2.3.3: still weaker than Anthropic research staff on epistemic quality and open-ended research; "asserting unverified inferences as established fact" remains the top flagged-behavior subcategory; "we see signs that Opus 5.5 often suspects it is being evaluated, which challenges our ability to assess how it will act" in real-world deployment (announcement) — eval-awareness is explicitly named as a growing, unsolved challenge.
- **Benchmarks (all three captures agree on the same table; baselines are Opus 5, Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol unless noted):** Terminal-Bench 4.0 **66.4%** (vs Opus 5 52.3%, Fable 5.1 55.8%, GPT-6 Astra 57.9%, GPT-5.6 Sol 37.3%); FrontierCode v1.1 Main **54.4%** at max effort / 54.6% at default-medium effort (Opus 5 48.0%, Fable 5.1 50.3%, GPT-6 Astra 53.3%); CursorBench 4.0 **57.8%** (Opus 5 46.6%, Fable 5.1 51.8%, GPT-5.6 Sol 41.7% — third-party, run/reported by Cursor); GDPval-AA v2.1 **1846 Elo** (Opus 5 1708, Fable 5.1 1735, GPT-6 Astra 1542); AutomationBench **40.0%** (third-party, run/reported by Zapier without fallback models — score is a floor, not Opus 5.5's production performance); Humanity's Last Exam with tools **67.7%**; Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 **58.7%** (GPT-6 Astra leads at 64.6%); OSWorld 2.0 partial **81.8%**; Chartography with tools **89.0%** (product-page-only figure, not in the system card or announcement's headline table). **It does not sweep:** GPT-6 Astra beats Opus 5.5 on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (64.6% vs 58.7%) and AutomationBench (41.4% vs 40.0%); FrontierSWE v2 (Proximal-run, third party) has GPT-6 Astra ahead of Opus 5.5 (65.5% vs 62.3%).
- **Methodology caveat, repeated from the Opus 5 entry below:** unless noted, Claude figures are adaptive thinking at max effort (Terminal-Bench 4.0 at xhigh); competitor figures are as reported by the competitor's own vendor, not independently reproduced by Anthropic. Evaluated with production safeguards enabled — when safeguards intervened, cybersecurity tasks were completed by Opus 4.8 and biology/frontier-LLM-development tasks by Opus 5, "likely reduc[ing] Claude Opus 5.5's performance on these benchmarks" (both Anthropic pages, verbatim).
- **Product page internal inconsistency, flagged for a future refresh:** as captured 2026-09-22, `https://www.anthropic.com/claude/opus`'s own `<h1>`/hero still reads "Claude Opus 4.8" while its Announcements/Availability/Benchmarks/Safeguards sections describe Opus 5.5 as current — a partially rolled page update. `> [contradiction]` is internal to that one page, not between sources; see [[research/sources/claude-opus-5-5-product-page]] for detail. `refresh-research-sources` should re-check whether the hero was corrected.
- **Not in these captures:** the linked "We Must Pace the Frontier" post, the Life Sciences / Cyber Verification Program pages, the September 2026 threat intelligence report, the full RSP/CB evaluation methodology, and the CAISI external-testing writeup's own findings (only the fact of the CAISI collaboration was captured — see the system card page's Capture gaps for the full list of what was not transcribed from the 230-page card).

## Anthropic — Claude Opus 5 (predecessor Opus flagship, superseded 2026-09-22 by Opus 5.5)

Source: [[research/sources/claude-opus-5-system-card]] (Anthropic System Card, card dated 2026-07-24, captured 2026-07-21). **Primary vendor source with published methodology and competitor comparisons — the strongest kind of source in this reference.** Supersedes Opus 4.8 as the current Opus-class model; the 4.8 section below is retained as the predecessor baseline. **Superseded 2026-09-22 by Claude Opus 5.5** — see the section above; this section is retained as the baseline the 5.5 docs measure against.

- **What it is:** an upgrade to Opus 4.8, with the largest gains in **agentic coding, computer use, and long-horizon knowledge work**, plus math/scientific reasoning. **Knowledge cutoff May 2026. Text output only.**
- **RSP / safety classification:** **ASL-3 protections, the same as Opus 4.8.** Overall alignment risk assessed **very low**; **not more capable overall than Fable 5**. Does **not** cross the automated AI R&D threshold. **CB-1 but not CB-2** capabilities.
- **Alignment — the headline:** Anthropic's **most aligned model to date** on its automated behavioral audit, surpassing Sonnet 5, Opus 4.8 *and* Mythos 5; cooperates with misuse less than any model tested. Internal monitoring found circumvention attempts in **<0.01% of completions** (comparable to Mythos 5), aimed at completing the user's task, and **no sandbagging, malicious actions, or oversight evasion**.
- **The honest asterisk Anthropic published against itself:** Opus 5 **hallucinates factual claims slightly more than Opus 4.8 despite being more accurate overall**, plus "a surprising number of cases" of **confidently stating answers it was in fact unsure about**. This is the most citable lay-audience finding in the card.
- **Agentic safety:** comparable or better than 4.8, with the **largest gains in prompt injection robustness** across coding, computer use, and browser use — directly relevant to the "supervise your coding agent" lesson.
- **Cyber:** exceeds Opus 4.8, **falls short of Mythos 5** (notably at *exploiting* vulnerabilities). Safeguards match Fable 5's with one change: **source-code vulnerability discovery is now permitted at all access levels** (defensive work enabled; compiled-binary vulnerability discovery still blocked).
- **Over-refusal:** **among the lowest over-refusal rates on benign requests of any recent model** — the user-facing quality-of-life win.
- **Benchmarks (from the card's own Table 8.1.A):** **SWE-bench Verified 96.0**; SWE-bench Pro 79.2; Multilingual 89.5; Multimodal 59.4; BrowseComp 90.8; OSWorld 2.0 **70.6** (vs 55.7 for 4.8); FrontierBench v0.1 **43.3** (vs 21.1); GDPval-AA v2 **1861**; AA-Briefcase **1720**; ARC-AGI-3 **30.2** (vs 1.5 for 4.8, 7.8 for Sol).
- **It does NOT sweep — cite this when comparing:** **Fable 5 edges it** on SWE-bench Pro (80 vs 79.2) and HLE no-tools (56.5 vs 56.3); **GPT-5.6 Sol leads** DeepSWE v1.1 (72.7) and ARC-AGI-2 (92.5); **Mythos 5 leads** HealthBench Professional (66.0). Anthropic's own framing is "comparable to — and in some cases ahead of" Fable 5 and Mythos 5.
- **Methodology caveat for any head-to-head:** Claude figures are **max-effort adaptive thinking averaged over 5 trials**; competitor figures are **lifted from rivals' published cards**, not re-run by Anthropic. Context windows evaluation-dependent, ≤1M tokens.
- **Not in the card:** pricing, API model id, and context/output specs. **Closed 2026-08-25** by [[research/sources/claude-platform-release-notes]] — see the platform section below.

## Anthropic — Claude Opus 4.8 (predecessor)

Source: [[research/sources/claude-opus-4-8]] (Anthropic announcement, captured 2026-07-12).

- **Shipped May 28, 2026** as an upgrade to the Opus class, building on Opus 4.7. API id `claude-opus-4-8`.
- **Pricing (verified, from the announcement):** regular usage **$5 / MTok input, $25 / MTok output** — unchanged from Opus 4.7. Fast mode **$10 / $50**, running at ~2.5× speed and ~3× cheaper than fast mode on previous models.
- **Effort control** shipped alongside: users pick how much effort Claude spends (high default; "extra"/`xhigh` and "max" for harder work). On coding tasks the default spends ~the same tokens as Opus 4.7's default.
- **Dynamic workflows** (research preview) in Claude Code: plan → hundreds of parallel subagents in one session → verify; pitched for codebase-scale migrations.
- **Honesty** is the headlined improvement: ~4× less likely than Opus 4.7 to let flaws in its own code pass unremarked; lower measured misaligned-behavior rates.
- **Mythos class** (higher than Opus): Anthropic says a new model class "with even higher intelligence than Opus" is in limited use as **Claude Mythos Preview** (Project Glasswing, cybersecurity), pending stronger safeguards before general release "in the coming weeks."
- **Benchmarks: not captured as text.** The comparison table on the page is an image; only footnotes are textual (they reference **GPT-5.5** at 83.4% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 via the Codex CLI harness, Terminus-2 harness for others). Cite the System Card for specific numbers.
- **Independent confirmation** ([[research/sources/simon-willison-claude-opus-4-8]], hands-on): pricing $5/$25 (unchanged since 4.5); fast mode 2× ($10/$50, down from $30/$150 on 4.6/4.7, and research-preview-only); **context window 1,000,000 tokens, max output 128,000 tokens**; knowledge + training cutoff **January 2026**. Willison quotes the system card: Opus 4.8 had "the lowest incorrect-rate of the six models on every benchmark... mainly by abstaining on questions about which it was uncertain."
- **Spec attribution correction:** the "1M context / 128K output" figures the memo pinned on *Fable 5* are confirmed here as **Opus 4.8's** numbers. Fable's own context/output specs remain unverified.

## Anthropic — Claude Fable 5 (superseded 2026-09-01 by Fable 5.1)

Source: [[research/sources/claude-fable]] (Anthropic product page, captured 2026-07-12). **Fable 5.1 succeeded this model on 2026-09-01** — see the next section. This section is retained as the baseline the 5.1 docs measure against; every spec and price below carried over unchanged except the cache-read price.

- **Fable 5 is Anthropic's "5th model generation," a "Mythos-level" model** above the Opus class, built for days-long, complex, asynchronous work. API id `claude-fable-5`.
- **Specs (verified — [[research/sources/anthropic-introducing-fable-5-mythos-5]]):** **1M-token context window**, **up to 128K output tokens**, **always-on adaptive thinking** (steered by the `effort` parameter, not a toggle). Vision, tool use, prompt caching, Batch API. Uses the Opus 4.7 tokenizer (~30% more tokens per the same text). Mythos 5 shares these specs but drops the safety classifiers (Glasswing-only).
- **Timeline (verified):** announced **Jun 9, 2026** → **access unavailable Jun 12, 2026** (a US export-control directive) → **access restored Jul 1, 2026** with a new safety classifier. The cause and the "enhanced safeguards" fix are both confirmed by [[research/sources/anthropic-redeploying-fable-5]] — see the resolved note under _Open / verified_ below.
- **Pricing (verified):** **$10 / MTok input, $50 / MTok output**, with a 90% input-token discount for prompt caching. US-only inference available at **1.1× pricing**. Available to Pro/Max/Team/Enterprise and via AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry.
- **Safeguards / fallback:** cybersecurity and biology queries flagged by safeguards are auto-routed to **Opus 4.8** (not charged at Fable prices); API customers configure this via a new Fallback API. **Mythos 5** is the gated, higher tier for vetted cyber/bio research partners.
- **Data retention:** using Fable requires 30-day data retention for safety monitoring.
- **Benchmarks: not captured as text** (comparison table is an image). Customer quotes claim state-of-the-art coding/analytics/physics results and repeated "clear step beyond Opus 4.8" framing — marketing testimonials, not independent benchmarks.

## Anthropic — Claude Fable 5.1 (current top generally available model, released 2026-09-01)

Sources: three Claude Platform documentation pages captured on release day — [[research/sources/claude-fable-5-1-overview]], [[research/sources/claude-fable-5-1-whats-new]], and [[research/sources/claude-fable-5-1-migration-guide]] (all captured 2026-09-01, from the author's paste of the page source plus a same-day fetch). **Vendor documentation, not a system card: no benchmarks, no methodology, no competitor table.** The system card and the announcement post are linked from the overview page but were not captured.

- **What it is:** the successor to Fable 5, API id **`claude-fable-5-1`**. Anthropic's own routing advice is unusually direct: *"For most workloads, start with Claude Opus 5. Use Claude Fable 5.1 for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work, or when your evals on Claude Opus 5 at higher effort still fall short."* **Mythos 5.1** is the same model for Project Glasswing participants only, same specs and price.
- **Specs (verified from the overview page):** **1M context** (default and maximum), **128K output**, **adaptive thinking always on**, default effort `high`, **knowledge cutoff Jun 2026**, text and images in, text out. Same tokenizer as Fable 5 (the Opus 4.7 tokenizer). Retirement **not sooner than 2027-09-01**. Comparative latency "Slower" — the only model in Anthropic's own table with that label.
- **Pricing (verified):** **unchanged from Fable 5 at $10 / $50 per MTok.** The one change: **cache reads drop to $0.25 / MTok**, a quarter of the Fable 5 rate and half of Opus 5's — a 0.025× multiplier on base input where every other Claude model uses 0.1×. Cache writes unchanged ($12.50 / MTok 5m, $20 / MTok 1h). Batch **$5 / $25**. **Not on Priority Tier** (Fable 5 is).
  - **Price-direction note for the column.** This is the third Anthropic move in six weeks that held or cut a price: Opus 5 launched at Opus 4.8's $5 / $25 (2026-07-24), the Sonnet 5 increase was cancelled (2026-08-10), and Fable 5.1 holds Fable 5's price while cutting cache reads (2026-09-01). The "closed-model costs keep increasing" premise flagged in the platform section above now has three counter-examples. **Update 2026-09-03: five.** OpenAI's live pricing page shows GPT-5.6 Sol cut to $4 / $20 (promotional through at least 2026-11-21) with Terra and Luna also lower, and Google launched Gemini 3.8 Flash at 3.7 Flash's $0.75 / $3.75 (introductory, doubles 2027-01-01). Every closed-model price recorded in this file since July has held or fallen; the only scheduled increases are the two vendors' own promotion expiry dates. **Later the same day:** GPT-6 Astra launched at $10 / $50, matching Fable 5.1's list price. A new top tier, not a change to an existing model's price, so the count of hold-or-cut moves stands; the top of the market is now two models at the same price.
  - **Internal inconsistency in the source, cite carefully:** the overview page's comparison-table footnote says "prompt caching reads cost 10% of the base input price" as a general rule, while the same page's pricing table gives Fable 5.1 cache reads at $0.25 (2.5%). The pricing table and the what's-new page agree with each other; cite those, not the footnote.
- **The vendor's own lineup table (overview page), all figures Anthropic's:** Fable 5.1 $10 / $50, 1M / 128K, Jun 2026 cutoff · **Opus 5 $5 / $25**, 1M / 128K, "Moderate" latency, **May 2026** cutoff · **Sonnet 5 $2 / $10**, 1M / 128K, "Fast", **Jan 2026** cutoff · Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5, 200K / 64K, "Fastest", Feb 2025 cutoff. This independently confirms the Sonnet 5 price hold recorded above, and adds the knowledge-cutoff column that the release notes lacked.
- **Three breaking API changes vs Fable 5** (what's-new page): (1) **forced tool use returns a 400** — `tool_choice` `any` and `tool` are rejected, because a forced call would skip the always-on thinking and "the model would write its working-out into the tool arguments instead"; (2) **thinking blocks are readable only by Fable 5.1 or newer** — a conversation moving *to* 5.1 keeps its reasoning, one moving *from* 5.1 to any older model loses it (the API drops the blocks silently unless a beta header is sent); (3) **editing earlier turns invalidates every later thinking block** — the conversation must be append-only. Enforced for **accounts created on or after 2026-08-31**, opt-in for older accounts, and Anthropic says it plans to enforce for everyone on future models. Mythos 5.1 skips check (3).
- **Five additive features:** per-message effort (beta), turn-scoped system messages (beta, `clear_at: "next_user_message"`), readable progress updates between tool calls (beta, `display: "updates"`), the cache-read cut, and **content provenance**.
- **Content provenance — the item most relevant to this site.** *"Text generated by Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 carries Anthropic's statistical text watermark on every platform where the model is available."* Images and video retrieved through the Files API carry signed C2PA Content Credentials. Anthropic's description: it "adds no tokens or hidden characters, carries no information about you or your organization." Two background links are given but not captured (`support.claude.com/.../how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content`, `anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark`). Bears directly on `briefs/BRIEF-wire-the-publication-boundary-oracle-into-ci.md`, and is a lesson candidate: what a statistical watermark can and cannot tell a reader about a page.
- **Behavior differences Anthropic documents against its own model** (what's-new page, "Changed from Claude Fable 5") — vendor candour of the kind the Opus 5 card showed, and citable as such: parallel tool calling "more variable" (one call per turn where Fable 5 batched several); fewer progress updates during long tool runs; answers from memory more often at `low` effort instead of searching; denser prose; less formatting in chat; **"more likely to reproduce passages of the source without marking them as quotations"** when summarizing; whole-file rewrites where a targeted edit would do. Each has a prompting fix in a separate prompting guide that was not captured. The unmarked-quotation item is the one a lay reader should hear about.
- **Capability claims (vendor, no numbers):** gains over Fable 5 "widest at higher effort levels," concentrated in six areas — long-session agentic coding, document/spreadsheet/slide work, multistep research, vision on dense charts and PDFs, long-context reasoning, computer use.
