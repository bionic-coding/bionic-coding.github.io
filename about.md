We're living through a shift I expect to prove bigger than the industrial revolution. Machines can now take over the *making* of things we assumed only people could make — software, most of all. The interesting question isn't whether they can. It's whether the human stays in control while they do.

That question is why this site is called what it is. **Bionic, not automatic.** *Bionic* means a person made more capable — augmented, still deciding, hands on the wheel — not a person replaced. The machine takes over the manufacturing; you keep the judgment it can't supply: what must be true, what must never break, and how you'd know. The augmentation runs the opposite direction from autocomplete — it doesn't write your code for you, it hands you the leverage and leaves you in charge.

That's the posture behind everything here. The easy thing to do with these tools is let go of the wheel — spray output at the world and hope. This site pulls the other way: toward keeping a human firmly in the loop, and treating AI-assisted work as **engineering**, not slop. That thread ties together the five things this site is.

- **[The Manifesto]({{ "/manifesto/" | relative_url }})** — the reason this site exists. One claim about software: **code is now disposable**, and we have to rethink how we build once that's true. It's my current thinking, written to be argued with — not a finished doctrine.
- **[Lessons]({{ "/learn/" | relative_url }})** — a plain-language field guide to modern AI, for people who want to understand the pieces without building models for a living. Start anywhere.
- **[Crux]({{ "/crux/" | relative_url }})** — an open-source system for giving AI coding agents durable project context: objectives, decisions, research, plans, and invariants that live with the code.
- **[Articles]({{ "/articles/" | relative_url }})** — thinking out loud: notes on what's new, a weekly read on the news, and whatever I'm working through.
- **[Tools]({{ "/tools/" | relative_url }})** — things I've built, and things I recommend, for doing this work the careful way.

## The manifesto, in a line

For fifty years we treated source code as the asset. It never was. The durable thing is *intent* — the invariants a system must honor, and the ability to regenerate the code from them on demand, cheaply and provably. The human owns those invariants; the machine owns the manufacturing. That's the line the whole method draws, and it's where control stays with you. ## About the author

 I build software with agentic tools and use this site to document the methods, failures, and useful tools I encounter. The lessons come from hands-on work. The news coverage draws on primary sources where possible and separates what I can verify from what a company or outside source claims.

This is a working notebook, not a claim to have the industry figured out. Recommendations can change as the tools change. You can follow the public work, including Crux, on [GitHub](https://github.com/bionic-coding).

## Editorial standards

- **Sources:** Factual articles link to their evidence. Primary sources come first when they are available.
- **Evidence labels:** News coverage distinguishes verified facts, vendor claims, outside reporting, and rumors. Opinion appears as opinion.
- **Hands-on advice:** Reviews and recommendations say when they come from direct use. Vendor material is not treated as independent proof.
- **Corrections:** Factual errors are corrected in place. Material corrections receive a dated note on the article rather than disappearing silently.
- **Disclosures:** Any sponsorship, affiliate relationship, free access, or vendor involvement that could affect a recommendation will be disclosed on the page.
- **Living lessons:** Lessons show when they were last reviewed so you can judge how current the guidance is.
