## Headlines

**1. `CHANGELOG.md` is live on the site, and you already built the detector that catches it.**
`garden-engineering-gap-publication-boundary-gate` · The file has no frontmatter, so Jekyll
copies it verbatim into `_site/` instead of rendering it. That is why it survived the
`exclude:` pass that caught the other three repo docs — that pass removed pages, and this is
a static file.

Here is the part worth your attention. PB-0001/RUN-002 built a durable oracle for exactly
this failure and recorded it: `find _site -type f ! -path '*/assets/*' ! -path '*/fonts/*'`,
with a baseline of 26 intended files. I ran it against the shipped build tonight.

| build | inventory count | expected |
|---|---|---|
| `main` at `daeef0d` | **28** | 27 |
| `garden/exclude-changelog-from-build` | **27** | 27 |

Twenty-six intended files plus your new post is 27. The build shipped 28. The oracle was
correct, it was written down, and nothing runs it. The same run snapshot records why: "no CI
gate for any V-unit (systemic — deferred)."
[[adrs/ADR-0005-serve-a-landing-homepage-and-a-five-item-top-nav]] carries the matching
line, and its Negative consequences predicted this in words: "Future publishable
repo-documentation files may be exposed unless someone adds them to that list."
`CHANGELOG.md` was created in the same cycle that wrote that sentence, and shipped nine days
later.

I drafted the one-line fix on `garden/exclude-changelog-from-build` and verified it both
ways: the file is gone from `_site` and the page count is unchanged at 25. That closes the
instance. The class stays open until something runs on every build, and I put the design
questions — count versus allowlist, fail versus warn — in
[[inbox/gardener-idea-publication-boundary-gate]]. A bare count fails on every new post, so
it would be disabled within a month; the allowlist is the version worth building.

I am not asking you to treat this as urgent. A published changelog is embarrassing, not
dangerous. I am asking you to notice the shape: this boundary has now failed twice, and both
times the cause was a hand-maintained list with no feedback signal.

**2. The annotated link is the column's signature, and it has thinned issue over issue.**
`garden-improvement-link-annotation-decay` · [[briefs/BRIEF-this-week-in-ai-format]] states
the rule plainly: "every link is annotated and dated. The anchor/primary source is bolded and
listed first." Here is what actually shipped.

| issue | bolded anchor | dated | annotated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 17 | yes | yes | yes |
| Jul 27 | yes | no | yes |
| Aug 03 | no | no | yes |
| Aug 14 | no | no | no |

This week's links are bare URLs with titles. Compare the Jul 17 issue, where a reader learns
from the list itself that The Register's piece notes Bruno Lemos had been defending the model
hours before it wiped his database. That line does work no bare link can do.

I do not think you decided to drop the convention. I think it is the first thing that goes
when you are writing fast, because it is the last step and the least fun. Two ways out: bold
and gloss only the anchor link per story and let the rest be bare, which cuts the work by
two-thirds and keeps the signal; or move the annotation into the template as a required
field so the empty gloss looks wrong while you draft. The second matches how you fixed the
pseudo-tag bug — you changed the scaffold so the hazard cannot recur quietly.

**3. Two stories from the week you just covered, one of which complicates your own lede.**
`garden-news-open-weights-and-pricing` · Your lede argues open weights are ascendant because
closed-model costs keep rising. The week gave you the strongest possible witness for the first
half and a direct problem for the second.

On Aug 10, Meta released **Muse Glimmer** — a 30B open-weight agentic model under Apache 2.0,
built to run agents on consumer hardware. That is your thesis in its strongest form, and it is
the "augmented, not replaced" argument in hardware: capability that lives on your own machine.
Coverage disagrees on the name, which is the column's "correct the popular misread" move
sitting there waiting. Details and the naming conflict are in
[[inbox/gardener-news-muse-glimmer]].

The same day, Anthropic cancelled the scheduled Sept 1 increase for Claude Sonnet 5; the
introductory $2/$10 per MTok becomes the standard price. Your post published four days later
and says closed-model costs keep increasing. That is a checkable claim with a counter-example,
so it is worth a correction beat. Both items are snippet-level from Perplexity, and neither
domain is accepted in [[garden/sources]], so I did not fetch either primary — confirm before
publishing. See [[inbox/gardener-news-sonnet5-price-hold]].

The stronger version of your argument does not rest on price at all. It rests on control and
locality, and Glimmer is the evidence for it.
