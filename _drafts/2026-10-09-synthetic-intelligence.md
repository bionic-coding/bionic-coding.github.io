I think Synthetic Intelligence is what I'd call where we are right now. Let me explain.

## Artificial intelligence

I really like the word artificial for where things started with LLMs, because it did feel artificial. Something tasted off a lot of the time.

A good analogy is artificial vanilla. Maybe it's good enough that most people can't taste the difference, but a chef can. Early LLM output often felt like that. It looked convincing until someone who knew the subject took a closer look.

## Synthetic intelligence

Synthetic is the word I'd use for where we are now. I like it because it implies that the intelligence has been synthesized.

Think about synthetic oil in a car. We use it because it does the job. In the same way, synthetic intelligence can now do some of the knowledge work we'd otherwise have to do ourselves.

Some is doing a lot of work in that sentence. We still have to decide what needs doing and whether the result is good enough.

But it's useful. And it's abundant.

I think the abundance is worth pausing on. You can ask for another approach, another draft, another check. You can give several agents different parts of a problem and have them work at the same time.

Used correctly, that abundance can make it feel like we've hit superintelligence. Work that would have taken you days can come back while you're doing something else.

But we're not there yet. Ten agents can still miss the same thing. Someone has to know what to check.

## Superintelligence

I think when we finally reach superintelligence, it won't be debatable. We'll see new discoveries at a rate beyond anything we've experienced.

That's the distinction I'm making. Abundant intelligence already changes how much work we can do. Superintelligence would change what we're capable of discovering.

For now, synthetic intelligence feels like the right name.
